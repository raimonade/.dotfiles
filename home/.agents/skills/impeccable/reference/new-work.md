# New work

For a genuinely new surface or an approved replacement of the visual world. A section, component, or state inside an established surface inherits that surface — build it there instead.

## 1. Decide what is already true

Read `DESIGN.md` when it exists, then representative code, tokens, components, and assets. One of four situations holds:

- **Established world** — inherit it. A missing `DESIGN.md` does not erase a coherent identity already living in the code.
- **Incomplete brand** — preserve confirmed assets and recognizable traits, then extend the system to this surface.
- **Replacement approved** — product truth, content, function, constraints, and explicit brand commitments survive; the visual world is replaced rather than polished.
- **No visual authority** — create a world, with the user.

## 2. Ask what changes the work

One round, two or three related questions, shaped by mode:

- **Persuade** — who must act, what must they believe, what real proof or assets can earn it.
- **Operate** — the task, the information it needs, the states that matter, the frequency and constraints.
- **Read** — the reader's question, the source material, the structure and wayfinding.
- **Experience** — what leads, how exploration unfolds, which interaction or transition matters.

Across modes: what success looks like, what must stay untouched, what would make a polished result still feel wrong. Skip anything the code already answers. A precise brief may need only a compact confirmation. With no one to answer, state assumptions and continue.

## 3. Choose the right amount of invention

**Extending an established surface.** Inherit its world and composition. Resolve only the new purpose, hierarchy, states, and interaction, and how the addition joins what surrounds it. Do not run a world exercise; do not rewrite `DESIGN.md`.

**A whole surface inside an established world.** Keep the visual system fixed and sketch materially different structures from the content, task, and real user behavior. Generate enough alternatives to escape the first plausible arrangement, then compare them by task clarity and fit.

**Creating or replacing the world.** Work outward from the subject, not inward from your defaults:

1. Name the product's unique mechanism in one sentence, the audience's real scene, its cultural home, and what this first surface must prove. Identify the page this category usually ships and its predictable opposite so neither becomes an unexamined default. A brief that paints its own picture may justify a literal direction when it serves the product; compare it with at least one meaningfully different interpretation when the axis remains open.
2. Generate concrete visual systems, artifacts, places, or rituals the audience knows, each with a reason it carries the mechanism. Include graphic traditions as well as physical objects: notation, publications, identity programs, data graphics, and familiar interfaces. Diversify when the candidates cluster in one material family; stop when the strongest distinct directions are clear.
3. Turn the strongest material into complete directions: each joins a reusable visual world to a concrete first-surface experience — first viewport, visitor path, signature interaction, cross-surface reach, and its honest risk.
4. When the choice materially changes the product, present a small set of genuinely distinct directions and let the user choose. A precise or narrow brief may need one grounded recommendation instead of artificial alternatives. Include a familiar category-standard option when it is a credible contender, evaluate it honestly, and keep bolder alternatives fully committed rather than blending them into a compromise.

A borrowed world is a working system, not a mood board. When one is chosen, carry its palette and material, type and composition, topology, controls, states, and responsive rules into the product — and when the source is itself an interface language, commit to its grammar across navigation, content, controls, and states.

Every direction must already be viable before it is offered: real palette and component family, a distinctive composition, one product-specific experience, workable at full scale inside the available assets and performance budget, and every relationship it visualizes true.

## 4. Commit the world

**Color strategy before colors.** Restrained (neutrals plus one accent — the default when the visitor came to operate or read), Committed (one saturated color carrying 30–60% of the surface), Full palette (3–4 named roles), or Drenched (the surface *is* the color). Color commits at page scale: fields that own regions, not accents sprinkled on a neutral ground. Light or dark is never a default — write one sentence describing who uses this, where, under what ambient light, and let that force the answer.

**Choose faces from the subject and mode.** Operate and Read are often well served by system stacks and workhorse UI faces; Persuade and Experience may benefit from a more distinctive display voice. Treat frequently generated choices—Fraunces, Playfair Display, Space Grotesk, Inter-as-display, DM Sans, Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans, Instrument Sans—as prompts to verify intention, not as forbidden fonts.

**Calibration.** Undirected AI interfaces cluster into a few looks whatever the subject: warm cream ground with high-contrast serif display and a terracotta accent; near-black with one neon accent and glowing edges; broadsheet hairlines with italic display serif and small tracked mono labels. Each is legitimate when the brief calls for it. Where the brief leaves the aesthetic free, landing in one means the self-check failed: if someone could guess your palette and type from the category alone — or from category-plus-avoidance — rework. A bookish or child-facing subject does not soften this; book cloth, thread, jackets, and shelf ephemera span the whole saturated spectrum, and cream paper is the smallest corner of it.

Energy is not the enemy of trust. A brief's negative constraints ("no gamification", "no hype") rule out those devices, not exuberance, and adjectives about the product's behavior ("calm coaching") do not dictate the surface's energy.

## 5. Record the decision

Before code, state the direction as a short working contract in the plan, task notes, or a local source comment where the repository accepts design rationale. Do not require design metadata to ship in production markup:

- **THESIS** — the one idea this surface owns, and the category-default arrangement it refuses.
- **OWN-WORLD** — palette and component language, specific enough to be recognizable with all content removed.
- **STORY** — what the visitor understands, believes, and does.
- **FIRST VIEWPORT** — the exact composition: what is where, at what scale, and where the primary action sits.
- **SIGNATURE** — the element, interaction, or motion moment that makes the surface recognizable, when one is earned.

If a block reads like a mood, the direction is not decided yet. Revisit this contract during the finish pass.

## 6. Build with full commitment

Land the first build fully committed. Committing is the hard part; the passes that follow exist to make the committed thing clear and effective, never to dilute it. The safe rendition is the known risk in unattended work.

- **The first viewport is a thesis, not a header.** Demonstrate the mechanism immediately, at the scale the form has in life. The memory test: if someone left after one viewport, what would they describe an hour later? If the honest answer is a mood, the concept has not committed.
- **Carry the world through the whole surface.** Nav, buttons, inputs, and links should express the chosen vocabulary while preserving established, accessible components when they already fit. Customize only where the direction earns it.
- **Prove, don't claim.** Show the subject doing its job: the interface at work, specifics a competitor could not copy-paste. A section that restates a claim in different words adds length, not substance.
- **Author the assets.** Great surfaces live on carefully made content — names, entries, copy, covers, textures. In greenfield work, author illustrative material at production fidelity and label it synthetic; commercial and factual claims (prices, customers, benchmarks, capabilities) stay uninventable and ship as clearly marked placeholders on the user's replacement list. Gradients, glass, and generic icon tiles where an authored asset belongs are the gap wearing chrome.
- **Build the form's real leverage.** When the chosen world names a technique — canvas, WebGL, view transitions, generative motion — build the technique, not a static imitation. The graceful fallback serves constrained clients; it is not the default experience.
- **Pace the scroll like a studio.** Vary density, scale, image, motion, and quiet inside one grammar. A dense passage earns a quiet one, and the page ends anchored by a real close. One spacing rhythm throughout, with more space above a heading than below it.
- **Use real, verified imagery when the brief implies it.** Inspect supplied and local assets for the subject's physical object rather than substituting category imagery; one decisive photo beats five mediocre ones. If the user authorizes external retrieval, let the host workflow perform it and verify any resulting URL before use.
- **Author motion as material when it has a job.** If motion carries identity, concentrate it rather than scattering hover effects. Static work is valid. Bound expensive effects and keep content visible by default. Details in [motion.md](motion.md).

Semantics, accessibility, performance, responsiveness, project conventions, and working behavior are preserved throughout — see [craft-floor.md](craft-floor.md).

## 7. Inspect and finish

Inspect in one batched round at the surface's real sizes: desktop and mobile on the web; the shipped device classes on native, captured as [platforms.md](platforms.md) describes. Critique the render against the request and the direction contract, fix every material gap in one batch, and confirm with one final round. Two rounds is the ceiling; further polishing belongs to a fresh review, not this thread.

On a Persuade surface, close by verifying the mode did its job: a first-time visitor knows what this is, why it matters, and what to do — within seconds, in the form's own vocabulary.

If the run established a durable visual system, offer to record it in `DESIGN.md` — written from the built result, never ahead of it. Keep it to what a future contributor must not re-derive: palette roles and values, type roles and scale, spacing and radius scales, elevation vocabulary, component character, imagery stance, motion grammar, and a short do/don't list. An ordinary extension does not rewrite `DESIGN.md`.
